## The acts at a glance

- **Act I — The Taking** *(Ch. 01–02, complete):* the egg in the ash and the wyrm out of the
  older burning; the chain forged in nine days; the collar set on a throat that lowered itself;
  the eleven-day walk into the Hall of Nine Keys. The cities catch a thing that let itself be
  caught.
- **Act II — The Verdict** *(Ch. 03–06, interior complete):* the Nine convene; Aldric kneels and
  names it *god*; the doors bar with no hand on them; the hall becomes a temple; the first true wing
  comes down from the north; the sealed eight learn what a god requires; and (Ch. 06) the attending
  breaks — *toward* the god — and the word of the kneeling escapes the bronze.
- **Act III — The Spread** *(under way, Ch. 07–10 drafted):* word of the kneeling gets out and
  runs the plain ear to ear, winning not by being believed but by being *argued against*; then the
  smell of smoke becomes the flame — the **older burning wakes**, comes up through the founders'
  well, and stands into the morning as a column of banked red light, so the nine cities wake to a
  fire in the west where no sun rises; the league is redrawn from a ledger of edges into a ring of
  nine warmths facing one fire, and reckons **there was never only one** (Ch. 09); then the oldest
  dread is remembered at last — the **Long Cold** named not as a war but as the nameless unfightable
  turning of the world against warmth that forged the league and made the cities throw the word away
  (Ch. 10). Still to come: the **eastern debt** comes due against a thing that cannot be warred.
- **Act IV — The Turning** *(planned):* belief becomes power; wings spread; the naming schism
  splits the league; Aldric becomes prophet, prisoner, or both.
- **Act V — The Verdict Returned** *(planned):* the core reversal is paid off — who was judging
  whom — and the cities kneel, break, or burn; then the aftermath and coda.

## Chapter beats (the load each one carries)

**01 · The Ashford Breach** — Opens on the egg in the deep warm ash of Ashford's fallen
meeting-hall. The wyrm rises from an *older burning* under the town, burns the spearmen (*it
bleeds*), speaks **three words** framed as an *introduction* (content withheld), and flies
*toward* the cities — "the way a letter goes toward the hand it was addressed to." The boy who
does not run; the egg gone, leaving a clean cold hollow, moving east.

**02 · The Taking of the Wyrm** — The Foundry Commission forges the great chain in nine days;
the westward column under grey-and-iron banners; **Aldric's first sight** of the wyrm. A lone
smith closes the collar on a head lowered to the dust — no battle: "the chain was on the
cities." Housed in the Hall by logistics (the pit was half-dug), not ritual. The egg follows
one night's travel back, and waits at the gate.

**03 · The Council's Verdict** — The Nine convene after forty years. The law offers three
findings — *beast, enemy, sovereign*. Aldric, who came to vote *enemy*, kneels and speaks the
unlawful fourth: **god**. The doors bar with no hand on them. The saga's thesis, dramatised.

**04 · The Temple That Was a Hall** — The barred hall becomes a temple. The eight each arrive at
a private name (Vessa's silence, Hedric's *enemy*, Sera's *specimen*, Peryn's *we kept the shelf
and lost the book*, Callenreth's *what comes after us*). The **three unnamed councillors are named**
(Ormund, Lisenne, Tobane); **Tobane's "it sees me"** finishes the turn. Outside, the **first
true wing** settles on the hill north of the gate; the beacon-chain is lit; the egg stirs and the
boy hears **three notes at once**.

**05 · The First Wing** — The city reckons with the wing on the northern hill and splits three
ways: **master, kin, or lure** (all three true, none provable). Inside, the eight learn what a
god requires — **attention**, freely then helplessly given: Hedric's eye slides off the wall,
Sera's measuring becomes devotion, Vessa's silence becomes gratitude; only Peryn keeps his eyes,
having paid early. At the gate the boy names the egg *the one that is coming* and feels it
**stir and test the size of its dark** — the third note rising from *answer* to *call*.

**06 · The Eight** — The **wearing**: attention is the most expensive thing a creature has, and a
room of nine cannot spend it forever (Callenreth loses his sentences; Ormund kneels always; Sera's
pages stop). The **fracture** breaks *toward* the god, not away: **Vessa**, past surrender, begins to
*want* — to be the one the god attends — envies Tobane's being-seen, breaks the temple's law and
demands *"Look at me,"* and the god obliges and **burns her single self** smooth into the temple's
first peace (*"a god sees you the way fire sees paper"*). Peryn: *"We were always going to hand
ourselves in."* Meanwhile the **word escapes through the frame, not the door** — engineers core the
founders' stone, Aldric's *"it is a god"* leaks up the crack, foreman **Corse** carries it ear-to-ear;
the deputies **lie** to the cities. At the gate the **fourth note** comes in and gathers the chord;
the hill-wing **lifts its head**.

**07 · Word of the Kneeling** — Act III opens. The escaped word is answered: the deputies' lie
meets the beacon-woken cities; belief begins to spread; the thing at the gate, now holding all four
notes, begins to move. The city's rumor and the boy's ash have not yet met. Under the noise the
**older burning wakes** and *reaches* — not toward the chained god in the well but toward the
*youngest* fire under the boy's hands at the gate — and the ground begins to warm.

**08 · The Ember-Drake Wakes** — The reaching *arrives.* The seam draws its last long breath and
lets it go as heat; the sealed quarter cracks along the line from **well to gate**; the town, leaning
**toward** and not away, comes to the lip of the parted stone and *faces* the red it cannot pray to.
The **ember-drake comes up through the founders' own well** — the door a people dug to read the future
and so left fit for the future to come through — passes the throne it does not take, warms the worn
eight at last, and **stands up out of the ground into the morning** as a column of banked red light,
unhurried as a planet; the nine cities wake to a fire in the west where no sun rises. At the gate the
**young note and the old note become one note in two sizes** (the child's voice finding the parent's
pitch); then the great note lifts away and the egg strikes alone again — *answered once and forever*,
no longer the lost child's song. The boy makes the hearth-tongue's promise — *"I'll keep you till it's
your turn. Hush now."* Seeds Ch. 09: the cities must answer what they have *seen*, and reckon there was
never only one.

**09 · The Nine Cities** — Pull back to the league. The ember-drake's standing goes out not up the
beacon-chain but **through the ground the cities stand on** — one note, **nine hearings**: barges knock
it down the River wharves, the Wallsmen's high tower finds nothing to war upon and leans *thin*, the
Coinhouse abacus slides west of the last column, the Kilnward's banked kilns draw their draught, the
Fenreeve's black water **rises** to it, the Marches say their kneeling lord's word at last in daylight,
the Archive's **lost book falls open mute** on the stone; Callenreth and Sera, seatless, hear it purest
through the temple floor. The map of the league **redraws itself** — from a ledger of edges into a ring
of nine warmths facing one fire — and the cities look down at the sleeping thing they were built upon,
then up, and understand the sentence that undoes every chain's arithmetic: **there was never only one.**
Under the gate the youngest fire strikes clean into a world it knows now is full of its kind, and the
boy is the one soul unafraid of the number — because he does not count the fires, he *keeps* them.

**10 · The Long Cold Remembered** — The oldest dread comes forward, because a people about to be asked
*how many more* must first remember what *once* was too many. The Long Cold is defined **in character,
not in a ledger**: not a war (it had no enemy, no army, no battle) but the **nameless unfightable turning
of the world against warmth** that huddled nine proud rival towns into one grudging league — bound *by
cold and debt more than by love* — and changed a people of **omen** into a people of **law**. Its length
stays **(unfixed)**: "a cold that long stops being an event and becomes a condition." **Sera Lowe**, the
only councillor born *after* the Cold, spent her life calling its inherited dread a *ghost* — and is worn
smooth understanding the grandparents were *right* and only had the century wrong. The **dry well** is
revealed as a **decision, not a ruin** — the founders' listening-instrument, kept still to read the deep
fire turn, let go dry when the new law chose **law over listening**; the god coils in it to be *read* by
heirs who forgot how. And the **lost word** was not lost but *thrown away* — "not because it was false,
but because it was true," the one truth no survivor of the Cold could bear to keep. Seeds Ch. 11: the
**eastern debt** comes due, against a thing no army can war.

## Recurring images & motifs (keep consistent)

- **The struck-bell / barred-door note** — one toll → three notes → (Ch. 05) a *chord* that
  *calls*. Binds captive, egg, and wing as one instrument "beginning to be played."
- **The four kneelings** — Aldric (grand), the boy at Ashford, the boy at the gate, Tobane
  (*it sees me*). The boy's, to the thing *not yet hatched*, is framed as "perhaps the first."
- **Tidal / geologic imagery** for the wyrms — "slow as a tide," "unhurried as a planet,"
  water finding the low place.
- **The lost word** — the old tongues' name for what the wyrms are, thrown away by the new law
  because it "could not be legislated." *God* is Aldric's new name over a genuinely lost one.
- **Section headers + an italic closing coda** in every chapter, the coda seeding the next.
