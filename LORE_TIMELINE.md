## The one pivot: the Great Arising

The whole saga hangs from a single nail. The book keeps **no calendar and no dates** — that is
deliberate (see §*Why the saga keeps no calendar*, below, and `TIMELINE §6`). Its clock is not
years-since-a-king but the distance between events, measured in **burnings, marches, watches, and
the memory of catastrophe.**

The one fixed point is the **Great Arising** — the burning of Ashford and the rising of the wyrm
*out of the ground.* Everything is placed relative to it: **Before the Arising (BA)** and **After
the Arising (AA)** **[scaffolding]**. The world itself has no such reckoning — which is a plot
point, not an oversight. If a calendar is ever wanted in-world, its natural zero is not a
coronation but the Arising: *the day the ground remembered its name.*

> **"The Great Arising"** is this file's canonical name for the event the chapters call *the
> Breach* — the rising of the wyrm from the older burning beneath Ashford (Ch.1–2). The two names
> are the same event: *Breach* is how the Free Cities, a magistracy of walls, name it (a wall was
> breached); *Arising* is how the saga names it from above. Both are on the spine.

## I. THE FOUR AGES

Zoomed all the way out, the saga stands in **four ages**, oldest to newest. Ch.1–4 — the entire
written manuscript — live wholly inside the *last* of them, in its first few weeks. The three
older ages loom behind the glass, pressing on the story.

### Age One — The Age of the Older Burning
*Pre-memory · (unfixed) duration · the wyrms sleep in the ground*

Before there were cities, before there was law, before there was memory: **a fire in the ground.**
The wyrms' true origin is *below* — they come **up, out of the ground**, out of "an older
burning… that Ashford had built its meeting-hall over and forgotten," not down from the northern
cold country *(Ch.1 §"What Came Before"; Ch.2 §"The Ground Remembers Its Name")*. Over ages the
burning is built over, ploughed, paved — "a grave that was also a door." Its buried seam threads
"down through the bones of the world," joined like a root system, sleeping "in the frost."

*No cities. No law. No memory. The oldest thing in the saga, and the thing that ends it.*

### Age Two — The Age of the Founders
*Pre-memory · the cities are raised · omens are read · the wyrms are named*

The **founders** of the Free Cities raise the **Hall of Nine Keys** and its **reflecting well**,
and "read the future in a pool of water" *(implied)* — divination once worked, or was believed to.
In this age the **old tongues** have **a word** for what the wyrms are *(Ch.4, Peryn)*. That word
is the saga's central lost thing. This is the age when the world still *knew* what was sleeping
under it.

### Age Three — The Age of the Long Cold
*Within reach of living memory at its tail · catastrophe binds the cities · the word is thrown away*

A world-binding catastrophe — the **Long Cold** — falls, and still holds the Free Cities together
"by memory… more than by love." Out of it comes the **new law**, which throws away the old-tongue
word for the wyrms "because it could not be legislated." The cities become a magistracy of *law,
not of omen*; the well runs dry; the founders' divination lapses; the world forgets what it once
named. **Sera Lowe is born at the very tail of this age** — the only councillor born *after* the
Long Cold, the living hinge between the old fear and the new law.

> The Long Cold is the oldest *datable* event in the saga, because a living person is measured
> against it. Everyone on the Nine remembers it except Sera Lowe. That is the only generational
> clock the book gives us. Its nature, length, and cause are **(unfixed)** — reserved for a later
> chapter (`AUDIT §4`; `GAP-MAP` Ch.09).

### Age Four — The Age of the Waking
*The present of the saga · the older burning is touched again · Ch.1–4 live entirely here*

The older burning is *touched* again, and the ground "begins to keep its accounts out loud." The
wyrm rises, the egg wakes, the first true wing comes down out of the northern cloud, and the
forgotten name comes due. The **entire written manuscript (Ch.1–4)** spans, in true time, only
**from the Great Arising to a handful of sealed days after** — a few weeks at most. The vast
things (the older burning, the founders, the Long Cold) are all *behind* the story, leaning on it.

| # | Age | Reach | What defines it |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **The Older Burning** | pre-memory (unfixed) | The wyrms sleep in the ground; a fire threaded through the bones of the world. |
| II | **The Founders** | pre-memory | The Free Cities rise; the Hall and well are built; omens are read; the wyrms are **named**. |
| III | **The Long Cold** | tail within living memory | Catastrophe binds the cities; the **new law throws away the word**; divination lapses. |
| IV | **The Waking** | the saga's present | The older burning is touched; the wyrm rises; the name comes due. *(Ch.1–4 live here.)* |

## II. THE WARS (and the not-wars)

The saga is a book *about* war that keeps refusing to *be* one — its horror comes from
understanding, not from battle. Honestly counted from the page, there are **two great conflicts**
on the spine, and the book takes deliberate care that neither is yet a war of swords.

### War the First — The Long Cold *(a war against the world, in memory)*
*Age Three · (unfixed) · behind the story*

The Long Cold is the older conflict — not army against army but the cities against the world
itself, and against the cold. It is remembered as the thing that *forged the league*: the Free
Cities are "held together by the Long Cold's memory and by mutual debt more than by love." Whether
it was war, plague, winter, or all three is **(unfixed)** by design. What is canon is its *effect*:
it made a magistracy of law out of whatever came before, and it threw the old word away. This is
the war the whole present order is built to have survived — "the magistracy outlived every threat
it was written to answer, and had never once answered one that breathed" *(Ch.1; Ch.3, Callenreth)*.

### The Forty Years' Silence *(the long peace between the wars)*
*Late Age Three into Age Four · ~40 years Before the Arising · stated*

Between the wars is a silence. The Hall of Nine Keys "had not been opened to full council in forty
years, and the hinges knew it" *(Ch.3)*. Across those forty years the beacon-chain is drilled but
never truly lit in earnest; the border levy farms "three seasons of four"; the plague-years ruin
Peryn of the Archive's hands *(implied placement)*. Peace, and the slow forgetting — the quiet in
which the world lets go of the word it once had for the thing sleeping under it.

### War the Second — The War of the Wings *(the war the saga is walking into)* **[scaffolding]**
*Age Four · the saga's present and future · beginning on the page*

The conflict the whole saga turns toward has **begun but not broken** on the written page. Its
canon opening beats, in order:

- **The Great Arising** *(Day 0)* — the wyrm rises and burns Ashford's garrison of **twelve
  border levy**; one spearman wounds it (*it bleeds*) and is the first to burn *(Ch.1)*. This is
  the first blood of the age — but it is a burning, not a battle: the wyrm does not war, it
  *arrives.*
- **The Taking** *(Act I close)* — the cities forge the great chain and set the collar, and there
  is **no battle**: the wyrm lowers its head to the dust and holds still *(Ch.2)*. "The chain was
  not on the wyrm. The chain was on the cities." The one moment that looked like a war of capture
  was a surrender the cities mistook for a victory.
- **The First True Wing** *(Act II)* — the **second wyrm** comes down out of the northern cloud on
  the wing, **circles the city twice**, settles on the bald hill north of the gate, lays its head
  toward the city, and **waits.** It does **not** attack. The **beacon-chain is lit** at last —
  "the only thing forty years of drill left [the watch-captain] able to do" — and the warning-fire
  runs hill to hill across the plain *(Ch.4)*. This is the first wyrm-on-city event of the age, and
  the book makes it, pointedly, *not yet a battle.*

Whether the War of the Wings becomes a war of arms, a war of belief, or a *judgment* is the saga's
open question — the README's promised reversal: "a council that convened to judge a creature and
found, too late, that the creature had convened to judge them." The name **War of the Wings** is
this file's **[scaffolding]** for the conflict that Hedric hungers for, that Aldric's kneeling
undercuts, and that the wing on the hill announces without yet joining. It is *arriving*, the way
the Arising arrived.

> **Canon caution for future chapters:** as of Ch.1–4 there is **no named battle** in the saga —
> only a burning (Ashford's garrison) and a settling (the wing on the hill). Do not retcon a war
> of swords into the past; the horror is that the cities have *never* fought the thing that is
> coming, and their whole law is built on the belief that they could.

## III. THE GREAT ARISING (Day 0) — the hinge of the whole book

The pivot. Days are counted from here in the ledger; ages turn on it in the spine.

- **The rising.** The wyrm comes **up out of the ground**, from the older burning beneath
  Ashford's meeting-hall — not down from the cold country. Ashford becomes "a field of grey shapes
  and standing wells" *(Ch.1)*.
- **The three words.** Moments after, the wyrm speaks **three words** to the ashes — heard by three
  survivors who never agree and never repeat them, framed later as *"not a threat… an
  introduction."* Their content is withheld from the reader **by choice, not ignorance** *(Ch.1)*.
- **The egg.** In the deep ash of the fallen meeting-hall lies **an egg** the size of a curled
  sleeping man, forge-warm, glowing under a boy's hand, sounding the same **barred-door bell-note**
  the Hall's great bar will make. After the Arising it is **gone**, leaving a clean cold hollow its
  exact shape, and it moves *east, toward the cities* *(Ch.1)*.
- **Why east.** The wyrm flies **toward** the cage, not away — "the way a letter goes toward the
  hand it was addressed to." It surrendered on purpose, "to be recognized" *(Ch.1; Ch.3, Peryn)*.

Everything before this is *the older burning pressing up*; everything after is *the age paying out.*
The Great Arising is where the four ages meet the four kneelings meet the two wars — the single
struck note from which the whole instrument begins to be played.

## IV. THE FOUR KNEELINGS — the saga's truest thread

The kneeling-motif is the clearest thread on the spine — braided out of order in the telling, set
straight here. (Full detail in `TIMELINE §4` and `LORE.md`.)

| In true time | Who | To what | Shown in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | **The boy of Ashford** (nameless, age 9) | the **egg** in the ash, ~3 days After the Arising | Ch.1 |
| 2nd | **The young outrider** | the **empty hollow** where the egg had lain | Ch.1 |
| 3rd | **The boy of the night-watch** | the **egg at the city gate**, the eleventh night | Ch.2 |
| 4th | **Aldric ban Sorel** | the **captive wyrm** in the Hall — names it a *god* | Ch.3 |
| 5th–7th | **Ormund, Lisenne, Tobane** | the wyrm in the well, over uncounted sealed days | Ch.4 |
| (re-counted) | **The boy of the night-watch, again** | the egg, the night the wing lands and it stirs | Ch.4 |

> **The planted paradox.** Ch.4 calls the night-watch boy's kneeling *"the fourth of the four…
> and perhaps, in the reckoning no ledger kept, the **first**"* — because he alone knelt to the
> thing **not yet hatched.** The saga's own timeline is thus **contested in-world**: which
> kneeling was first depends on whether you count the chained god or the unhatched one. This spine
> records event order; the book reserves the right to argue meaning order.

## V. THE ONE-LINE SPINE

> **Older burning → the Founders name the wyrms → the Long Cold throws the name away → forty
> years' silence → the Great Arising (egg + three words) → the boy kneels → the nine-day forging →
> the eleven-day road (egg one night behind) → the wyrm led into the Hall → the Nine convene →
> Aldric kneels, names it *god*, the doors bar → the uncounted sealed days → the first true wing
> comes down, the beacon lights, the egg stirs, the boy hears three notes at once.**

Four ages behind the glass; two wars, one remembered and one arriving; one Arising at the hinge;
four kneelings braided through. Ch.1–4 are the first weeks of the fourth age — **the Age of the
Waking.**

## Why the saga keeps no calendar *(a note, not a gap)*

The absence of dates is **thematic** (`AUDIT §3.6`). The Free Cities are a magistracy of *law*,
which counts warrants, ledgers, and years-of-office — and the horror of the book is that the thing
coming for them **does not keep those books.** The wyrm's clock is the older burning's clock:
*ages, not years.* So the saga measures time in marches and burnings and watches, never in dates —
because to date a thing is to file it, and the saga's thesis is that this thing *cannot be filed.*

This file therefore uses **Before / After the Arising** only as scaffolding for the reader. The
natural in-world zero, if one is ever wanted, is the Arising itself — *the day the ground
remembered its name.* Any future dating system should hang from that nail.
