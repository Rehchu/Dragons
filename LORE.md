*A working canon reference for the great dragon saga. Everything below is drawn strictly

## Named characters — The Nine

The full council is the **Nine**. Nine councillors are referenced; **six are named**, and
their positions and stances are established. Three seats are alluded to but not individuated.

| Councillor | Office / faction | Established stance in Ch. 3 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High Warden Callenreth** | Presiding High Warden; grey-and-iron robes | Opens council; names the three lawful findings; does *not* himself vote on the page | Old man; the magistracy "outlived every threat it was written to answer." Feels the tide turn. |
| **Vessa of the River Guilds** | River Guilds | Votes **beast** — "I do not kneel to crows." First to panic when the doors bar. | Spends words "like a coin she resented spending." |
| **Peryn of the Archive** | The Archive | Reframes the creature as having *surrendered to be recognized* | Plague-scarred ("ruined hands," "the plague-years"); does not rise; the moral weathervane of the scene. |
| **Hedric of the Wallsmen** | The Wallsmen (military) | Wants **war** — hungrily, to be "necessary again" | Speaks of levies, the beacon-chain, and the eastern baronies' debt in men. |
| **Sera Lowe** | Youngest of the Nine | Wants **study** — cages, measurements, the creature as specimen | The only councillor born *after* the Long Cold. |
| **Aldric ban Sorel** | Third-chair; Keeper of the Southern Marches (a marcher lord) | Came intending to vote **enemy**; instead **kneels and names the wyrm a *god*** | The pivot of the chapter and (implied) the saga. |

Also present but **not named**: "two of the guild-wardens" who want the problem to be
someone else's; plus wardens and scribes as background. That leaves at least **three of the
Nine unnamed / uncharacterised** — a canon gap, not a contradiction. *(implied)*

## The Free Cities

- A **league of nine guild-and-warden magistracies** (per README). *Note: the README says
  "nine … magistracies"; the council is also nine — whether "one city, one councillor" is
  intended is* (unexplained) *and should be pinned before Ch. 1–2.*
- Held together "by the Long Cold's memory and by mutual debt more than by love."
- Factions established by name through their councillors: **River Guilds, the Archive, the
  Wallsmen**, and the **Southern Marches** (a marcher/border holding). "Guild-wardens" exist
  as a class. **Eastern baronies** owe the cities a debt "in men." *(all on the page)*

## Places

- **The Hall of Nine Keys** — seat of full council; opened "only in gravest need"; "built to
  hold judgment." Bronze doors that scream when opened; a great bar that drops into a bronze
  cradle. At its heart, a **reflecting well** (a dry marble basin) where the founders once
  "read the future in a pool of water" / "read omens in still water." Had not convened full
  council in **forty years** before Ch. 3.
- **The Ashford breach** — where the wyrm was taken. Ashford spearmen wounded it ("it bleeds")
  before it burned them. The creature flew *toward* the breach, not away. *(the taking itself
  is reserved for the unwritten Ch. 1–2)*
- **The northern cloud** — the direction from which "the first of the wyrm's true wings comes
  down" (Ch. 4 teaser). *(setup, not yet paid off)*

## History / the world's spine

- **The Long Cold** — a past catastrophe or age that binds the cities by memory; Sera Lowe is
  the only councillor born after it. Nature, duration, and cause are *(unexplained)*.
- The **founders** built the Hall and practised omen-reading in the reflecting pool — a lost
  or diminished art. *(implied that divination once worked / was believed)*
- The **new law** replaced the "old tongues." The old tongues had a **word** for what the
  wyrms are; the new law "threw it away because it could not be legislated." That discarded
  word is the saga's central mystery. Aldric supplies his own answer — **god** — but whether
  that is *the* old word or merely his naming is *(unexplained, and looks deliberate)*.

## The Wyrms

- **Not beasts, not enemies, not kings.** The saga's thesis, stated in the README and dramatised
  in Ch. 3: the creature is a "third thing" the law has no slot for.
- Physical: enormous; coils into the well "like smoke pouring backward into a bottle"; eyes
  "the color of a furnace seen through smoked glass"; radiates forge-heat; can **burn** men
  and **bleeds** when wounded. Moves rarely and slowly ("slow as a tide," "unhurried as a
  planet").
- **It can speak.** It spoke **three words** at the breach — words the survivors "could not
  agree on and would not repeat" — and has not spoken since. Content of the three words is
  *(unexplained — a planted hook)*.
- **It surrendered on purpose.** It flew toward the cage; "it could have unmade [it] with one
  breath." Peryn's reading: it surrendered "to be recognized."
- **The doors.** At Aldric's naming, the barred doors of the Hall close and bar **with no hand
  on them**. Whether the wyrm did this, or the Hall did, or naming itself did, is left open —
  Peryn notes "the creature has not moved."
- **"True wings"** — Ch. 4 teaser implies the council-hall wyrm is not alone; more are coming
  from the north. Number, kind, and relationship to the captured wyrm are *(unexplained)*.

## The law's three findings (the chapter's engine)

The law provides exactly three verdicts for the creature:

1. **Beast** — put down by any strong enough hand (Vessa's vote).
2. **Enemy** — warred upon by the united cities (Hedric's vote; Aldric's *intended* vote).
3. **Sovereign** — treated with, as with a king.

Aldric's turn is to reject all three as "small human words" — three doors onto the same room —
and to speak the unlawful fourth: **god**. This is the load-bearing idea of the whole book.

## The Ashford Breach — Chapter One additions

*New canon promoted from `chapters/01-the-ashford-breach.md`.*

- **The egg in the ash.** At the heart of burned Ashford, in the deep warm ash of the fallen
  **meeting-hall**, lay an **egg** the size of a curled sleeping man — ridged, dark, faintly
  translucent at its crown, forge-warm, glowing when the boy touched it. It emits the same
  **struck-bell / barred-door note** the Hall's great bar makes in Ch.3 (a deliberate echo).
  After the breach it is **gone**, leaving a clean cold hollow its exact shape; it moves *east,
  toward the cities*, one night's travel behind the outriders. Its relationship to the captured
  wyrm is *(unexplained — a planted second-wyrm hook, distinct from the Ch.4 "true wings")*.
- **The boy.** A nameless nine-year-old who returns to burned Ashford, finds and touches the
  egg, does **not run**, and kneels beside it — explicitly rhymed with Aldric's kneeling.
  Found dead among the garrison line by the outriders; his name is deliberately never recorded.
- **The wyrm's origin.** It came **out of the ground** — out of an *older burning* Ashford
  had built its meeting-hall over and forgotten — not down from the northern cold country.
  It may have walked among Ashford "in some smaller borrowed shape" first *(implied, disputed
  by the in-world histories)*.
- **The three words = an *introduction*.** The surviving witness (unnamed here) tells a scribe
  the words were "not a threat… an **introduction**," and that *understanding them is the whole
  of the harm*. The scribe is **Peryn of the Archive** — canonically tying Ch.1 to Ch.3, and
  establishing that the words' content is known to at least one dead witness but withheld from
  the reader by choice, not by ignorance.
- **Why it flew *toward* the cage.** Dramatised, not explained: it flies east "the way a letter
  goes toward the hand it was addressed to." Reinforces Peryn's Ch.3 reading (*surrendered to be
  recognized*) without resolving agency — still *(unexplained by design)*.
- **The outriders / the cities' banner.** The Free Cities ride under **grey-and-iron banners**
  (matching Callenreth's grey-and-iron robes) — first on-page confirmation the magistracy's
  colours are grey and iron.
