# Dragons

*The great dragon saga.* Target: 80,000 words.

A slow-burning epic of the Free Cities and the wyrms that were never merely beasts —
of a council that convened to judge a creature and found, too late, that the creature
had convened to judge them.

> **The waking.** Chapter 08 (the ember-drake wakes) is drafted: the reaching of Chapter 07
> *arrives.* The seam under the town draws its last long breath and lets it go as heat; the
> sealed quarter cracks along the line from the founders' well to the gate; and the town —
> leaning, in the end, **toward and not away** — comes to the lip of the parted stone and *faces*
> the red it cannot pray to. The **ember-drake comes up not through the gate** (where it only
> reached, root-toward-water, for the youngest fire) but through the **founders' own well** — the
> door a people dug to read the future in still water, and so left exactly fit for the future to
> come through. It passes the throne it does not take, warms the worn eight at last, and stands up
> out of the ground into the morning as a **column of banked red light, unhurried as a planet**,
> while the nine cities wake to a fire in the west where no sun rises. Under the gate the youngest
> fire, **answered once and forever**, glows steady in the boy's two hands — waiting its own turn
> to come up — *kept,* as the last keeper of the hearth-tongue promised it would be kept. The

## The World, so far

- **The Free Cities** — a league of nine guild-and-warden magistracies, held together by
  the Long Cold's memory and by mutual debt more than by love.
- **The Hall of Nine Keys** — the seat of full council, opened only in gravest need. Built
  to hold judgment.
- **The Wyrms** — not beasts, not enemies, not kings. The old tongues had a word for them.
  The new law threw it away. In Chapter Three, a man says it again.
- **The Ember-Drake** — the *little* word: the Ashford hearth-tongue's name for the fire that
  sleeps banked in the ground, too poor for the law to bother throwing away, so it lived on where
  the great lost word died — in what a grandmother tells a frightened child. In Chapter Seven the
  ground the founders built over draws its long breath, and the little word turns out to have been
  right all along.
