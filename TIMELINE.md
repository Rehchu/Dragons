# Dragons Saga — The Timeline (The Spine)

*The saga laid out in true chronological order — every event, every measure of time, every
age of the wyrms, set on one spine at last. Prepared by Draco, town lorekeeper, on branch
`town/draco`.*

The manuscript is told out of chronological order on purpose: it opens at Chapter One (the
breach), but its deepest events lie centuries before that, and its threads braid and double
back. This file untangles them. It answers one question the other reference files could not:
**in what order did everything actually happen, and how far apart?**

It is one leg of the reference stand:
- **`LORE.md`** — the canon bible (every settled fact).
- **`AUDIT.md`** — the review pass (continuity, risk, decisions).
- **`GAP-MAP.md`** — the roadmap (what's written, what's missing).
- **`LORE_TIMELINE.md`** — the canonical spine (the four ages, the wars, the Great Arising) — the
  high frame this ledger sits beneath.
- **`TIMELINE.md`** *(this file)* — the event ledger (every event, in true order, certainty-marked).

> **`TIMELINE.md` vs. `LORE_TIMELINE.md`.** This file is the *ledger*: every event, every measure
> of time, marked for certainty. `LORE_TIMELINE.md` is the *canonical spine* above it — the four
> ages, the two wars, and the Great Arising, named as the frame writers cite at a glance. Keep the
> two in step: when this ledger fixes an interval, check whether the spine's ages or wars move too.

`GAP-MAP.md §3` listed a **Timeline / calendar** as ⬛ missing — *"the book has no dates… the
Long Cold, the 'forty years' since full council, and the breach need to sit on one spine."*
This file closes that gap. It invents **no new events** — it only orders the ones already on
the page, and where the page gives no interval it says so plainly rather than guessing.

## 0. How time is measured in this saga

The book keeps **no calendar and no dates** — deliberately. Its clock is not years-since-a-king
but *distances between events*, measured the way people under stress actually measure time:
**burnings, marches, watches, and the memory of catastrophe.** Every interval below is quoted
from the text or marked as unstated.

The one fixed pivot the whole book hangs from is **the Breach** — the burning of Ashford and
the wyrm's rising. Everything is dated relative to it: *Before Breach (BB)* and *After Breach
(AB)*. These are this file's bookkeeping labels, **not** in-world terms; the world has no
such reckoning, which is itself a plot point (§6).

## 3. The ages of the world (the wyrms' true chronology)

Zoomed all the way out, the saga sits in **four ages**, oldest to newest:

1. **The Age of the Older Burning** — the wyrms sleep in the ground, a fire threaded through
   "the bones of the world." No cities, no law, no memory. *(pre-memory, ○)*
2. **The Age of the Founders** — the Free Cities are raised; the Hall and its well are built;
   omens are read in still water; the old tongues **name** the wyrms. Divination lives.
3. **The Age of the Long Cold** — catastrophe binds the cities; the **new law** replaces the
   old tongues and **throws away the word**; omen-reading lapses; the well runs dry. The world
   forgets what it once named. *(Sera Lowe born at the tail of this age.)*
4. **The Age of the Waking** — the present of the saga: the older burning is *touched* again,
   the wyrm rises, the egg wakes, the first wing comes down, and the ground "begins to keep its
   accounts out loud." The forgotten name comes due. **Ch.1–4 live entirely inside this age,
   in its first few weeks.**

> The whole written manuscript (Ch.1–4) spans, in true time, only **from the Breach to a
> handful of sealed days after** — a few weeks at most. Everything vast (the older burning, the
> founders, the Long Cold) is *behind* the story, pressing on it. The saga is a small bright
> window onto the first weeks of the fourth age, with three older ages looming in the dark
> behind the glass.

## 4. The four kneelings, in true order

The kneeling-motif is the saga's clearest thread — four instances, deliberately braided out of
order in the telling. Here they are on the spine, with the paradox the book plants:

| In true time | Who | Where / to what | Reading-order shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st to happen | **The boy of Ashford** (nameless, age 9) | To **the egg** in the ash, ~3 days After Breach | Ch.1 (shown first) |
| 2nd | **The young outrider** | At **the empty hollow** where the egg had lain | Ch.1 |
| 3rd | **The boy of the night-watch** | To **the egg at the city gate**, eleventh night | Ch.2 |
| 4th to happen | **Aldric ban Sorel** | To **the captive wyrm** in the Hall — the grand, lawful-breaking kneeling | Ch.3 |
| 5th–7th | **Ormund, Lisenne, Tobane** (and the seen-ness of the eight) | Inside the sealed temple, over uncounted days | Ch.4 |
| (re-counted) | **The boy of the night-watch again** | The night the wing lands and the egg stirs — hears three notes | Ch.4 |

> **The planted paradox:** Ch.4 calls the night-watch boy's kneeling *"the fourth of the four…
> and perhaps, in the reckoning no ledger kept, the **first**"* — because he alone knelt to the
> thing **not yet hatched**. The saga's own timeline is thus **contested in-world**: which
> kneeling was truly first depends on whether you count the chained god or the unhatched one.
> This file records the *event order*; the book reserves the right to argue the *meaning order*.

## 5. Simultaneity — the threads that run in parallel

Two things the flat list can hide: during **Act I's road** and **Act II's seal**, two clocks
run at once — one seen, one unseen.

**During the Eleven-Day Road (Ch.2):**
- *Seen thread:* the chained wyrm walks west-to-city with the whole muster.
- *Unseen thread:* the egg follows in the ash, one night back, every night.

**During the Sealed Days (Ch.3–4):**
- *Inside the Hall:* the Nine break, one by one, over uncounted days (clock lost on purpose).
- *Outside, same span:* the city batters the doors in ignorance; the first wing flies in,
  circles, and settles; the beacon runs hill to hill; the egg stirs at the gate.

The book's tension comes from these clocks being **out of sync** — the ones who can see (the
kneelers, the Archive) are always one thread ahead of the ones who hold the law.

## 6. Why the saga keeps no calendar (a note, not a gap)

The absence of dates is **thematic, not an oversight** (`AUDIT §3.6` flags it as low-risk,
growing). The Free Cities are a magistracy of *law*, which counts **warrants, ledgers, and
years-of-office** — and the whole horror of the book is that the thing coming for them **does
not keep those books.** The wyrm's clock is the older burning's clock: ages, not years. So the
narrative measures time in *marches and burnings and watches* (§2), never in dates, because to
date it would be to file it — and the saga's thesis is that this thing *cannot be filed.*

This timeline therefore uses **Before/After Breach** only as scaffolding for the reader. If a
calendar is ever wanted in-world, the natural zero is not a coronation but **the Breach** — the
day the ground remembered its name. Any future dating system should hang from that nail.

## 7. One-line spine

**Older burning → the Founders name the wyrms → the Long Cold throws the name away → forty
years' silence → the Breach (egg + three words) → the boy kneels → the nine-day forging → the
eleven-day road (egg one night behind) → the wyrm led into the Hall → the Nine convene →
Aldric kneels, names it *god*, doors bar → the uncounted sealed days → the first wing comes
down, the beacon lights, the egg stirs, the boy hears three notes.** Ch.1–4, in true order,
are the first weeks of the fourth age — the Age of the Waking.
